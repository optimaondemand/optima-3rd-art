🧰 The Songwriter's Toolkit
The building blocks you'll use all year. Come back here anytime.
① THE SONG MAP — HOW MOST SONGS ARE BUILT
VERSE 1PRECHORUSVERSE 2PRECHORUSBRIDGECHORUS
VerseTells the story. New words each time — details, scenes, what happened.
Pre-chorusBuilds tension. A short ramp that makes the chorus feel like a payoff. (Optional!)
ChorusThe big idea. Same words every time. Usually holds the hook and the title.
BridgeThe plot twist. A new angle, a new feeling, or a new sound before the last chorus.
② RHYME TYPES

Perfect rhyme — same ending sound.
light / night  ·  stay / away

Slant rhyme — close, but not exact. Sounds natural!
home / alone  ·  mind / time

Internal rhyme — rhymes inside the line.
“I ran as fast as I can through the rain”

Multi-syllable rhyme — two or more sounds match.
falling apart / calling my heart

③ WRITING A HOOK THAT STICKS

🎯 Keep it short. A hook is often 2–6 words.

🔁 Repeat it. Say it more than once in the chorus.

👀 Show, don't tell. “Your jacket's still on my chair” hits harder than “I miss you.”

🗣️ Sing it out loud. If it's hard to say, it'll be hard to sing.

🏷️ Make it your title. Most hooks are the song's name.

④ THE SONGWRITER'S PROCESS
1. SPARK
Catch an idea: a feeling, a phrase, a memory. 2. DRAFT
Write messy. Don't fix anything yet. 3. SHAPE
Find the hook, tighten rhymes, count syllables. 4. SHARE
Get feedback, revise, and release it!

🎙️ House Rules of the Studio
★ Every idea gets a fair listen. Rough drafts are welcome here.
★ Give feedback with Glow & Grow: one thing that works, one thing to try.
★ Write your own words. Borrow inspiration, never someone else's lyrics.
★ Keep it school-appropriate — songs can be deep without being inappropriate.
★ What's shared in the club stays respectful in and out of the club.
