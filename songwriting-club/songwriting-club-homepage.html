LINER NOTES
Welcome to the club! 🎶

Every song you love started as a very simple, maybe incomplete idea. Maybe it was a line someone overheard, a feeling they wanted to put into words, a beat tapped on a desk.

The goal of this club is to:

1equip you with the tools that musicians use,

2create a low pressure, creative environment for you to gain experience in writing songs/music, and

3have fun using our creative powers as we learn from each other.

You don't need to sing well or play an instrument. You just need to be ready to learn and ready to make mistakes. I’m an undergraduate Music student at Utah State, and I’ve always loved listening to music and singing songs. Once I started creating my own, I began to love it all more.

— Mr. Nickolas Birchum

① THE SONG MAP — HOW MOST SONGS ARE BUILT
VERSE 1PRECHORUSVERSE 2PRECHORUSBRIDGECHORUS
VerseTells the story. New words each time — details, scenes, what happened.
Pre-chorusBuilds tension. A short ramp that makes the chorus feel like a payoff. (Optional!)
ChorusThe big idea. Same words every time. Usually holds the hook and the title.
BridgeThe plot twist. A new angle, a new feeling, or a new sound before the last chorus.
② RHYME TYPES

Perfect rhyme — same ending sound.
light / night  ·  stay / away

Slant rhyme — close, but not exact. Sounds natural!
home / alone  ·  mind / time

Internal rhyme — rhymes inside the line.
“I ran as fast as I can through the rain”

Multi-syllable rhyme — two or more sounds match.
falling apart / calling my heart

③ WRITING A HOOK THAT STICKS

🎯 Keep it short. A hook is often 2–6 words.

🔁 Repeat it. Say it more than once in the chorus.

👀 Show, don't tell. “Your jacket's still on my chair” hits harder than “I miss you.”

🗣️ Sing it out loud. If it's hard to say, it'll be hard to sing.

🏷️ Make it your title. Most hooks are the song's name.

🎙️ Quick House Rules
★ Every idea gets a fair listen. Rough drafts are welcome here.
★ Give feedback with Glow & Grow: one thing that works, one thing to try.
★ Write your own words. Borrow inspiration, never someone else's lyrics.
★ Keep it school-appropriate — songs can be deep without being inappropriate.
★ What's shared in the club stays respectful in and out of the club.
🧭 4 Club Guiding Principles
🎨 Just create.
You may think you need to know everything about music before starting, but that’s not how that works. Jumping into it will give you experience that will enhance everything you learn and make those things easily applicable. That’s how you learn how to swim, that’s how you learn a new language, and that’s how you become a great songwriter.
🙌 Encourage.
We’ll all be sharing a lot of our creations in this club. Maybe we’re all beginners. Maybe we’re all bad at it right now. Encouragement fuels motivation. And what are we going to do without any motivation? Yes, there will be times for constructive criticism here, but criticism is best given when paired with compliments.
🧱 AI.
This group is meant to give us a space for us to use our creative powers and to build things ourselves. Things we can call our own. For this reason, AI should not be used to make music for us. However, it can be a useful tool for things like finding rhymes, giving prompt ideas, and explaining musical principles. Think of it like Legos. The whole point of buying a Lego set is so YOU can build it yourself.
😜 Don’t take yourself too seriously.
Make mistakes. Be cheesy. Experiment. It’s supposed to be fun, which means some things need to be funny.
